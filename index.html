Wicker

Nature gives nothing for free. Every scrap of power your Cunning Woman wields is bought with a life: your own people at the forest altar, captives dragged home by the Oss, whole crowds in the wicker. Burn too many of your own and the rest will flee into the woods.

How to play

Touch

  • Tap your people to select them. Tap the ground to move them, or an enemy to attack.
  • Drag with one finger to pan. Pinch to zoom, and twist two fingers to turn the world.
  • Turn on Box, then drag to select a group. Double-tap a unit to select all of that kind nearby.
  • To clear your selection, tap with two fingers at once, press and hold on empty ground, or tap the selection line above the tabs.

Mouse and keyboard

  • Left-click selects your people, or tells the selected ones what to do; drag to box-select. Right-click on empty ground clears the selection; right-click on something still commands.
  • Right-drag turns the view, middle-drag or WASD pans, the wheel zooms. Space finds your Cunning Woman, keys 1–0 pick spells, F toggles full screen, Esc cancels.

Your tribe

  • Villagers chop wood, build, and go into huts to breed. Breeding is the heart of everything, because the only mana is what you give back to nature.
  • The forest altar takes one offering at a time and is stronger the more trees stand around it. The wicker man takes five and burns, giving mana and a frenzy of speed. The Wicker God takes twelve and burns for an enormous offering, terrifying any enemy who sees it. Both wicker figures burn down and must be rebuilt.
  • Burning your own people fills the dread meter. Past the mark, frightened villagers start slipping away into the wilds. Dread fades with time, and faster with more huts.
  • Osses, from the Oss House, roam the ground around where you last sent them and seize anyone they can catch, wildmen or enemy, carrying them home to your thrall fold at a run. Once an Oss has a captive it will not take orders until it is home. Captives are worth far more than your own people and cause no dread; kill an Oss on its way home and its captive goes free. You start with one thrall fold, which holds fifteen. Two or more captives left together in a fold will breed, slowly at first and then faster, so a fold left alone fills itself.
  • Herne's Oak is a vast dead tree raised in deep woodland. Hang your Cunning Woman from it for two minutes, during which she can do nothing, and the Wild Hunt rides for you: antlered horsemen who roam the whole world killing anyone they find outside the reach of an enemy Cunning Woman. Every death they cause feeds you as richly as a captive, and lost riders return.
  • Build a Maypole and idle villagers will dance around it, easing your people's dread. After enough merriment a May Queen is crowned. The longer she lives, the more her offering is worth, up to a vast sum with no dread and a long frenzy. Select fighters and tap her to swear them as her guards; you can do the same for your Cunning Woman. Rival Osses will hunt her, and a captured Queen can be offered by her captors for her full worth.
  • Blackthorn hedges are cheap thorny barriers. Choose the hedge, tap where it starts and where it ends, and villagers plant it a length at a time. Nothing walks through a finished hedge, your own people included, so leave gaps; enemies must go round or hack through, which turns open ground into chokepoints.
  • Your Bog is a hidden trap: your own people cross it safely, but enemies cannot see it and march straight in. It lasts until it has drowned ten of them. Fighters near their own buildings also take slightly less damage.
  • When a rival army sets out for you, a horn sounds and the marching column shows on your map through the fog. An attack that loses half its strength breaks and runs home.
  • The Rook Tree is a lookout in a dead tree. It sees a long way and hurls fire-pots at any enemy balloon in range, bringing one down in three hits. It ignores everything on the ground.
  • Send fighters to your oak, your tomb or a hollow hill and they are posted there as guards. Guards stay put and are left out when you select your army.
  • A tribe that loses every building, trained unit and its Cunning Woman is finished: its last villagers are dragged off as captives by whoever broke it. A tribe whose people are all dead hands its empty buildings to the conqueror, with an offering of mana.
  • Ground where a building has been razed is salted: for three minutes its old owner cannot build there. A rival that falls to a fifth of its greatest size is broken. Its people gather at their stone circle where everyone can see them, its halls fall in and its Cunning Woman does not return; destroy its last fighters and the rest are yours.
  • Tap a rival's marker at the top of the screen to look at where its people are. Tap again to move on to the next group.
  • Training buildings turn a villager into a warrior, archer, Oss or balloonist.
  • To cross water, have your Cunning Woman raise a Land Bridge from the shore towards the land you want. Anyone can walk it, your enemies included.
  • Balloonists drift above the fighting and drop charges. Only other balloonists can reach them.
  • Wildmen wander the wilds. They make easy captives.
  • Hollow hills lie out in the wilderness. Bring your Cunning Woman to one with twenty followers and at least 1,500 mana and she is sealed inside for good. The hill becomes a wicker fortress: from it she sees everything and casts anywhere at half the mana. If enemies tear it down she is lost for a minute, then rises at your stone circle, and half your mana goes with her.
  • All-against-all needs an unlit wicker man, ten followers to burn and a full bar of mana. Every hut falls, and every soul walks to the Moot at the world's heart to fight to the end.
  • Pick a spell and tap a target. Your Cunning Woman walks into range and casts. If she dies she returns at your stone circle after forty-five seconds, as long as your tribe survives.
  • You win when every rival tribe has no followers or buildings left.
